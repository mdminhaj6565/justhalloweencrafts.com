The easiest spooky Christmas ornaments to make are stuffed felt bats, black cat silhouettes in felt, skulls painted on shatterproof baubles and pipe cleaner spiders. All four use cheap craft supplies, take under an hour each, and hang from an ordinary ornament hook or a loop of thin ribbon. Mix them in with your usual decorations for a creepy-cute tree, or give them a small black or white tree of their own.

If you already made Halloween decorations this year, many of them can come back out in December with a little red, green or gold added. Below you will find the supplies, step-by-step instructions for each ornament, ideas for making them look festive rather than purely Halloween, and tips for hanging and storing them so they last. For a baked version, see our salt dough Halloween ornaments recipe.

What You Need

  • Black, grey, white and red craft felt (wool-blend felt is softer and holds stitches better)
  • Embroidery floss or thick thread, a needle and sharp scissors
  • A little polyester stuffing or cotton wool
  • Clear or white shatterproof (plastic) baubles
  • Black and white acrylic paint or paint pens, a fine brush and a pencil
  • Black pipe cleaners (chenille stems) and small craft eyes or beads
  • Thin ribbon, baker's twine or metal ornament hooks
  • Fabric glue or a low-temperature glue gun
  • Small accents: gold thread, red felt for tiny Santa hats, mini bells, glitter (optional)

How to Make a Stuffed Felt Bat Ornament

A felt bat ornament is two bat shapes stitched together around the edge with a little stuffing in the body.

  1. Draw a bat about 4 inches (10 cm) wide on paper: an oval body, two pointed ears and scalloped wings. Fold the paper in half before cutting so both wings match. Our bat pom pom tutorial includes a wing shape you can trace.
  2. Pin the paper template to two layers of black felt and cut both pieces together.
  3. Sew two tiny white felt circles or small beads to the front piece for eyes. Do this before joining the layers so the knots are hidden inside.
  4. Fold a 5-inch (13 cm) piece of ribbon in half and tuck the cut ends between the layers at the top of the head.
  5. Blanket stitch or running stitch around the edge with grey or silver floss, starting at one wing tip and passing through the ribbon to secure it.
  6. When you reach the body, stop with a gap about 1 inch (2.5 cm) wide, push in a small amount of stuffing with a pencil, then stitch the gap closed and finish the wings.

For a festive touch, glue a tiny red felt Santa hat between the ears or stitch the edge in gold thread instead of grey.

How to Make a Black Cat Felt Ornament

A black cat ornament is a flat felt silhouette glued onto a contrasting felt circle, so it reads clearly from across the room.

  1. Cut a circle about 3.5 inches (9 cm) across from red, green or white felt. A jar lid or mug makes a good guide.
  2. Draw a simple sitting cat on paper: a round head with two triangle ears, a pear-shaped body and a long curled tail. Keep it smaller than the circle.
  3. Cut the cat from black felt and glue it to the center of the circle with fabric glue. Press it flat under a book for a few minutes.
  4. Add two small yellow or green felt eyes, or dots from a paint pen.
  5. Glue the finished circle onto a second, slightly larger circle in a different color for a neat border, sandwiching a ribbon loop between the layers at the top.
  6. Leave it to dry flat; check the glue bottle for the full drying time before hanging.

How to Paint a Skull Bauble

To make a skull bauble, paint a white or clear plastic ball with a simple skull face in black: two large eye sockets, a small upside-down heart for the nose and a row of teeth.

  1. Use shatterproof plastic baubles, not glass. They are lighter, safer around children and pets, and easier to hold while painting.
  2. If the bauble is clear, paint the outside white with two thin coats of acrylic, letting each dry. White baubles can skip this step.
  3. Lightly sketch the face in pencil. Place the eyes just above the middle line of the ball so the skull looks forward when it hangs.
  4. Fill the eyes and nose with black paint or a paint pen, then add a curved mouth line with short vertical lines across it for teeth.
  5. Once dry, add details if you like: small flowers around the eyes in the style of our sugar skull pattern, a sprig of holly at the top, or a thin gold outline.
  6. Seal with a clear spray or brush-on varnish so the paint does not chip in storage.

How to Make a Pipe Cleaner Spider Ornament

A pipe cleaner spider is four bent pipe cleaners twisted together in the middle, with a bead or pom pom body glued on top.

  1. Hold four black pipe cleaners together and twist them once around their middles to make eight legs.
  2. Bend each leg down at the "knee" and out again at the "foot" so the spider can grip a branch.
  3. Glue a large black bead or pom pom on top for the body and add two small craft eyes.
  4. Either clip the spider straight onto a branch by curling its legs, or twist a loop of silver thread around the middle so it dangles.

Spiders look especially good hanging below a cobweb made of thin white thread, or sitting on our Perler bead spider web.

Tip: Make the spooky set feel like Christmas by choosing a limited palette. Black and white ornaments with a touch of red, or black with gold, look deliberate on a tree, while too many bright Halloween oranges and purples tend to clash with fairy lights and tinsel.

More Ideas for a Creepy-Cute Tree

Once you have a few ornaments, a handful of extra touches pulls the theme together.

  • Bead skulls: fused bead skulls from our Halloween Perler bead patterns become ornaments with a ribbon loop through the top row.
  • Pinecone critters: painted pinecone bats and owls from our pinecone crafts sit naturally among evergreen branches.
  • Salt dough shapes: cut ghosts, moons and bats with cookie cutters and paint them black and silver.
  • Garland: string felt bats on twine between ordinary baubles.

Safety Note

Small eyes, beads and pom poms can come loose and are a choking hazard, so keep these ornaments on the upper branches if you have toddlers or pets, or use stitched or painted eyes instead. Supervise children with needles and glue guns, and keep a low-temperature gun for kids' projects. Felt and stuffing burn, so do not hang ornaments against hot bulbs or near candles.

What is a spooky Christmas tree called?

People often call it a creepy-cute, gothic or "Creepmas" tree. It is usually an ordinary Christmas tree decorated with Halloween-style ornaments such as bats, skulls, spiders and black cats, often in a black, white, red or gold color scheme. Some people use a black artificial tree for extra contrast.

Can I use my Halloween decorations on a Christmas tree?

Yes, as long as they are light enough to hang. Small felt, paper, bead and salt dough decorations work well. Add a festive detail such as a tiny Santa hat, a red ribbon or gold thread so they blend in, and keep heavy or fragile pieces for shelves and mantels instead.

What is the easiest spooky ornament for kids?

The pipe cleaner spider is the easiest: children twist four pipe cleaners together, bend the legs and glue on a pom pom body and eyes. It takes about ten minutes and needs no sewing or cutting. The black cat felt ornament is a good next step for kids who can use scissors.

A few handmade bats, cats and skulls are enough to give a whole tree a playful, spooky twist, and they make thoughtful gifts for friends who love Halloween all year. When the holidays are over, pack them away with the tips in how to store Halloween decorations, and they will be ready for October too.
