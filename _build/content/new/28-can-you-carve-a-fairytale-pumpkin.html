Yes, you can carve a fairytale pumpkin, but it is harder work than carving an ordinary jack-o'-lantern pumpkin. Fairytale pumpkins have a tough, ribbed rind and thick, dense flesh, so you need a sturdy serrated saw, a smaller design and more patience. For many people, shallow surface carving, painting or simply displaying the pumpkin whole is the better choice.

Below you'll find what makes a fairytale pumpkin different, a step-by-step method for carving one, easier no-cut ideas, and why you may prefer to cook it once the decorating season is over.

What Is a Fairytale Pumpkin?

A fairytale pumpkin is a flat, deeply ribbed pumpkin, usually sold under the French heirloom name Musquée de Provence. It belongs to Cucurbita moschata, the same species as butternut squash, not to the field pumpkins usually grown for jack-o'-lanterns. The rind starts dark green and turns a warm tan-orange as it ripens, which is why it looks like the carriage pumpkin in an old storybook illustration.

Seed suppliers describe the flesh as thick, firm and deep orange. Johnny's Selected Seeds, for example, lists an average fruit weight of 15 to 40 lb for Musquée de Provence, so even a medium one is heavy to move once it is carved and lit. Plan where it will sit before you start cutting.

What You Need

  • A fairytale pumpkin with no soft spots, cuts or mold
  • A sturdy serrated pumpkin saw or a long serrated bread knife
  • A strong metal spoon or ice cream scoop, plus a large bowl
  • A washable marker or a printed paper template and tape
  • A linoleum cutter, clay loop tool or small craft gouge for surface carving
  • A cutting board or old towel to stop the pumpkin from rocking
  • Battery LED candles or a string of fairy lights

Step 1: Pick the Right Spot to Cut

Choose a flat, wide panel between two ribs for your design, because that is where the flesh is easiest to cut through. The deep ribs are thicker and uneven, and designs drawn across them look distorted when lit.

  1. Turn the pumpkin until the smoothest, widest section faces you.
  2. Check it sits steady; if it rocks, take a very thin slice off the base or rest it on a folded towel.
  3. Draw a simple design inside that one panel: two eyes and a mouth, a moon and stars, or a small door and windows like our pumpkin fairy house.

Step 2: Cut the Opening From the Back or Base

Open a fairytale pumpkin from the back or the bottom rather than the top, because the wide, flat top is hard to cut neatly and makes a lid that falls in.

  1. Mark a circle on the back, or on the base if you want to set the pumpkin over a light.
  2. Saw around it slowly with short, firm strokes, angling the blade slightly inwards so the piece can sit back in place.
  3. Expect each cut to take longer than on a normal pumpkin; let the saw do the work rather than forcing a knife.

Step 3: Scoop and Thin the Wall

Scoop out the seeds and strings, then thin the wall behind your design so the light can shine through. This is the most important step with a fairytale pumpkin.

  1. Pull out the seeds and fibers with your hands and the spoon. Keep the seeds if you want to roast or save them.
  2. Scrape the inside wall behind the design until it is noticeably thinner than the rest. A thinner wall is far easier to cut and glows more when lit.
  3. Keep the scraped flesh in the fridge if you plan to cook it the same day.

Our tutorial on how to hollow out a pumpkin has photos of the scooping stage.

Step 4: Carve the Design

Carve from the center of the design outwards, cutting small sections at a time so the wall stays strong.

  1. Push the tip of the saw through the line and use short up-and-down strokes.
  2. Remove each piece by pushing it gently inwards from the front.
  3. Keep at least a finger's width of pumpkin between cut-outs so the design doesn't break.
  4. Add finer details, such as eyebrows or brickwork, by scraping through the rind only with a lino cutter instead of cutting all the way through.

Tip: If the flesh is too thick to cut cleanly, switch to surface carving for the whole design. Scraping away the tan rind reveals the bright orange flesh underneath, which looks striking in daylight and glows softly with a light inside once the wall is thin.

Easier Ways to Decorate a Fairytale Pumpkin

You don't have to cut all the way through to make a fairytale pumpkin look special. These options take less effort and keep the pumpkin whole for longer.

  • Surface carving (etching): scrape patterns, leaves or a face into the rind with a lino cutter. The orange flesh shows against the tan skin.
  • Paint: acrylic paint or paint pens work well on the smooth panels between the ribs. See our no-carve pumpkin decorating ideas for designs.
  • Stack and group: the flat shape makes a stable base for a stack of smaller pumpkins, gourds and leaves on a porch or table.
  • Fairy door: glue a small wooden or card door and windows to one panel for a fairy house look without carving.
  • Lantern base: set a glass jar with an LED candle on top, like our mason jar lanterns.

How Long Does a Carved Fairytale Pumpkin Last?

A carved fairytale pumpkin starts to soften within days, just like any carved pumpkin, because the cut flesh dries out and is exposed to mold. A whole, uncut fairytale pumpkin keeps much longer: produce suppliers say it can be stored for several months in a cool, dry place, which is one reason it is so popular for autumn displays.

To slow down a carved one, keep it out of direct sun, bring it indoors or into a cool spot overnight, and use LED lights instead of a candle. Our guide on how to make a carved pumpkin last longer explains what helps and what doesn't.

Should You Cook It Instead?

If you'd like to eat your fairytale pumpkin, don't carve it for display. It is a well-known cooking pumpkin with sweet, smooth flesh that suits soup, roasting and pie, and keeping it whole means it stays fresh until you are ready to use it.

A carved pumpkin that has been sitting out with a candle inside, or that shows any mold or soft patches, should go in the compost rather than the kitchen. For more ideas on what to do once the season is over, see what to do with pumpkins after Halloween.

Safety note: The thick rind makes knives more likely to slip. An adult should do all cutting, work on a stable surface, cut away from your hand, and let children draw the design, scoop seeds or paint instead.

Can you carve a fairytale pumpkin?

Yes. A fairytale pumpkin can be carved, but its tough rind and thick, dense flesh make it slower and harder than an ordinary carving pumpkin. Use a sturdy serrated saw, cut a simple design on the flattest panel between two ribs, and thin the wall from the inside before you cut through.

Are fairytale pumpkins good for jack-o'-lanterns?

They make unusual, eye-catching jack-o'-lanterns, but they are not the easiest choice. The deep ribs distort large designs and the thick walls let less light through. Small faces, surface carving or a single cut-out shape on one panel usually look better than a big traditional face.

What is the difference between a fairytale pumpkin and a Cinderella pumpkin?

Both are flat, ribbed pumpkins sold for autumn decorating. The fairytale pumpkin usually means Musquée de Provence, a tan to orange-brown Cucurbita moschata. "Cinderella pumpkin" is often used for Rouge Vif d'Etampes, a bright red-orange French heirloom, though shops sometimes use both names loosely.

Can you eat a fairytale pumpkin after carving it?

Only if you cook the flesh straight away, on the day you carve it, and keep it chilled until then. Once a carved pumpkin has been displayed at room temperature, especially with a candle inside, it is safer to compost it than to eat it.
