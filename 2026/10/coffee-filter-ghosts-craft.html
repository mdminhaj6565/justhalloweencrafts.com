How to Make Coffee Filter Ghosts: Easy Kids Craft

By Just Halloween Crafts. Published October 7, 2026

Coffee filter ghosts are a classic Halloween craft for a good reason: they cost almost nothing, they take only minutes, and children of nearly every age can make them. With a few round paper filters, some cotton wool and a black marker, you can create a whole crowd of friendly little spooks to hang in windows, string into a garland or float above a party table.

This guide shows you how to make a basic coffee filter ghost step by step and then shares several ways to take the idea further, from glowing ghosts to colorful watercolor spirits.

Supplies for Coffee Filter Ghosts

  • White basket-style (round, fluted) coffee filters
  • Cotton wool balls, scrunched tissue or a small foam ball for the head
  • White string, thread or thin elastic bands
  • Black permanent marker or felt-tip pen
  • Scissors
  • Optional: liquid starch or a mix of white glue and water, watercolor paints, googly eyes, fishing line, LED tea lights

Basket filters give the best ghostly ruffle. Cone-shaped filters can be used too if you trim them into a rough circle first.

How to Make a Coffee Filter Ghost: Step by Step

Step 1: Make the Head

Roll two or three cotton wool balls together, or scrunch a piece of tissue, into a ball about the size of a large marble. This will form the ghost's head. For bigger ghosts, use a small foam ball or several layers of tissue.

Step 2: Cover the Head

Flatten one coffee filter and place the cotton ball in the center. Gather the filter down around the ball so it is fully covered and the edges hang down like a sheet. For a fuller ghost, use two filters stacked together.

Step 3: Tie the Neck

Wrap a piece of string or a small elastic band around the filter just below the ball and tie it firmly. This shapes the head and lets the rest of the filter flare out like a flowing robe. If you want to hang the ghost, leave a long tail of string or thread a loop through the top of the head with a needle.

Step 4: Draw the Face

Use a black marker to draw two eyes and a mouth. Round "O" mouths look surprised, wavy lines look spooky and big smiles look friendly. Press lightly, as the marker can bleed into the paper. Googly eyes are a fun alternative for younger children.

Step 5: Shape the Robe

Gently pull and fluff the edges of the filter so the ghost looks as if it is drifting. You can snip small notches around the hem for a tattered look.

Optional: Stiffen Your Ghost

For a ghost that stands up on its own, dip the shaped filter in liquid starch or a mix of equal parts white glue and water. Drape it over a small bottle or jar topped with the cotton ball head and leave it to dry overnight. Once dry and stiff, lift it off and add the face.

Creative Coffee Filter Ghost Ideas

Ghost Garland

Make eight to ten ghosts and tie them at intervals along a length of string or ribbon. Hang the garland across a fireplace, doorway or window. Mix them with paper bats or our easy tissue paper garland for a fuller look.

Floating Window Ghosts

Tie each ghost to a length of clear fishing line and hang them at different heights from a curtain rail. They twist gently in the air and look as though they are floating, especially when the window is lit from inside.

Glowing Ghosts

Drape a stiffened ghost over a battery-powered LED tea light. The paper diffuses the light into a soft, eerie glow that looks lovely on a windowsill or party table. Always use LED lights rather than candles with paper crafts.

Watercolor Spirits

Before assembling, let children paint the flat filters with watercolors in purple, green and orange, or drip on diluted food coloring. Coffee filters soak up color beautifully and create tie-dye patterns. Once dry, make them into ghosts for a bright, playful twist.

Ghost Treat Toppers

Instead of a cotton ball, place the filter over a round lollipop and tie it at the neck. Draw on a face and you have instant party favors. Keep these for older children, and remember that some guests may have food allergies.

Ghost Mobile

Cross two sticks or wooden dowels and tie them at the center, then hang ghosts from each end with thread of different lengths. Balance the mobile by sliding the ghosts along the sticks until it hangs level.

Tips for Making Coffee Filter Ghosts with Kids

  • Pre-cut the string into lengths before you start, so little hands do not have to wait for scissors.
  • Help with tying: younger children can hold the head in place while an adult ties the neck.
  • Use washable markers for very young crafters, and cover the table in case of bleed-through.
  • Keep small parts away from under-threes: googly eyes and cotton balls can be a choking hazard.
  • Make it a group activity: coffee filter ghosts work brilliantly for classrooms, clubs and parties because the supplies are cheap and the results are quick. See our guide to hosting a Halloween craft party for kids for planning tips.

Storing Your Ghosts

Coffee filter ghosts are light and fragile, so store them flat in a shallow box between layers of tissue paper. Stiffened ghosts keep their shape better if you store them upright in an egg carton. Keep them somewhere dry, as damp will make the paper limp.

Coffee Filter Ghost FAQs

Which coffee filters work best?

Round, fluted basket filters are the easiest to use because they already have a ruffled edge that looks like a flowing sheet. Unbleached brown filters can be used too, and they make great "old, dusty" ghosts for a haunted house theme.

Can I make them without cotton wool?

Yes. A scrunched ball of tissue, newspaper, a small pom pom or even another coffee filter rolled into a ball all work well as the head. For a weightier ghost that hangs straighter, use a small foam ball.

How long do starched ghosts take to dry?

Most stiffened ghosts are dry within 8 to 12 hours in a warm room. Thicker layers of glue mix can take up to a day. Do not try to speed up drying with a hairdryer held close, as it can blow the ghost out of shape.

Can coffee filter ghosts go outside?

They are best kept indoors, as rain and damp will make the paper soggy. For a porch display, hang them in a sheltered spot or inside a window where they can be seen from outside.
