How to Make a Hooded Cape from Fleece (No Hemming)

By Just Halloween Crafts. Published November 16, 2026

To make a hooded cape, cut one rectangle of fleece for the cape body and two mirror-image pieces for the hood. Sew the curved back seam of the hood, gather or pleat the top edge of the cape until it is exactly as long as the bottom edge of the hood, then stitch the two together and add a closure at the neck. Because polar fleece does not fray, you can leave every edge raw, so there is no hemming at all. On a sewing machine the whole cape takes about an hour.

This is the quickest way to get a hooded look for a witch, wizard, elf or Red Riding Hood, and it is simpler than a circle cloak because the body is a straight rectangle. If you want a long, swirling floor-length cloak instead, follow our step-by-step guide on how to make a hooded cloak.

What You Need

  • Polar fleece or anti-pill fleece, 60 inches (150 cm) wide: about 2 yards (1.8 m) for an adult knee-length cape, about 1.5 yards (1.4 m) for a hip-length cape or a child's cape
  • Matching thread (polyester thread suits fleece)
  • Fabric scissors or a rotary cutter and mat
  • A tape measure, sewing clips or long pins, and chalk or a fabric marker
  • A large sheet of paper for the hood shape (newspaper or wrapping paper is fine)
  • A sewing machine with a universal or ballpoint needle, or a needle for hand sewing
  • A closure: a hook-and-loop strip, a large snap, a cloak clasp or two fleece ties

Why Fleece Works for a Hooded Cape

Fleece is the easiest fabric for a hooded cape because its cut edges do not unravel, so you can skip hemming the front edges, the bottom edge and the hood opening.

It is soft, holds the shape of a hood well and washes without fuss, though it is warm for an indoor party. If you are still choosing a material, our comparison of the best fabric for a cloak or cape covers velvet, cotton, wool and more.

Step 1: Take Your Measurements

You need four numbers: the cape length, the cape width, and the height and depth of the hood.

  1. Cape length: measure from the bump at the back of the neck down to where you want the cape to end (see the table above).
  2. Cape width: hold your arms straight out to the sides and measure from wrist to wrist. For most adults this is close to the 60-inch width of the fleece, which means you can use the full width of the fabric and cut no side edges at all.
  3. Hood height: measure from the point where the neck meets the shoulder, up over the top of the head, and down to the same point on the other side. Divide by two and add 2 inches (5 cm) of ease.
  4. Hood depth: measure around the head from the middle of the forehead to the back of the head at ear level. Add 3 inches (8 cm) so the hood is roomy and does not pull at the forehead.

Step 2: Draw the Hood Shape

A simple hood is a rectangle with one rounded corner, cut twice as a mirror pair.

  1. On the paper, draw a rectangle as tall as the hood height and as wide as the hood depth.
  2. Choose one top corner to be the back of the head. Round it off with a big, smooth curve that starts about a third of the way down the back edge and ends about a third of the way along the top edge.
  3. Label the straight front edge "face opening" and the bottom edge "neckline", then cut out the paper shape.
  4. Hold the paper against the wearer's head to check the size before you cut fabric.

Want a dramatic point instead of a rounded back? Use the shape in our guide on how to make a pointed hood. For more detail on hood sizing, see how to make a hood for a cloak or cape.

Step 3: Cut the Fleece

Cut the cape body as one rectangle and the hood as two pieces from the fleece that is left.

  1. Cut a rectangle that is as long as your cape length and as wide as your cape width. If you use the full fabric width, you only need to cut across the top and bottom.
  2. Round the two bottom corners with a large plate as a guide so they hang neatly and do not flip up.
  3. Fold the remaining fleece in half, pin the paper hood shape to it and cut through both layers. This gives you a left and a right hood piece automatically.

Step 4: Sew the Hood

Sew the two hood pieces together along the curved back seam only, leaving the face opening and the neckline open.

  1. Place the hood pieces right sides together and clip along the curved back edge, from the top front corner down to the neckline.
  2. Stitch with a 1/2 inch (12 mm) seam allowance and a slightly longer stitch than usual, around 3 to 3.5 mm, which stops fleece from puckering.
  3. Trim the seam allowance on the curve to 1/4 inch (6 mm) so the hood sits smoothly. You do not need to finish the edges.
  4. Turn the hood right side out. For a neater face opening, fold the front edge back by 1 inch (2.5 cm) and topstitch it.

Step 5: Gather the Cape to Fit the Hood

The top edge of the cape must be exactly as long as the bottom edge of the hood, so you gather or pleat it to match.

  1. Measure the neckline of the sewn hood from one front corner to the other. This is your target length.
  2. Mark the center of the cape's top edge and the center back seam of the hood with a pin.
  3. Either run two rows of long gathering stitches across the top edge and pull the threads, or fold pleats about 1 inch (2.5 cm) deep from the center outwards.
  4. Adjust until the top edge matches the hood neckline, spread the fullness evenly, and stitch across it to hold the gathers in place.

Step 6: Attach the Hood and Add a Closure

Join the hood to the cape with the right sides together, then add a closure at the front of the neck.

  1. With the cape right side up, place the hood on top with its outside facing the cape, matching the center pins and the two front corners. Clip all the way along.
  2. Stitch with a 1/2 inch (12 mm) seam allowance, going slowly over the gathers.
  3. Try the cape on and decide where the closure should sit. Sew on a hook-and-loop strip, a large snap or a cloak clasp at the neckline corners.

Our guide on how to fasten a cloak or cape compares clasps, ties and frog closures in more detail.

Safety Note

For children, use our kids' cape sizes and a closure that pulls apart, such as hook-and-loop tape, and never ties or cords knotted at the neck. Keep children's capes above the knee so they do not trip, and take them off for climbing and playground play. Push hoods back when crossing roads, because they block side vision. Fleece is usually polyester, so keep any cape away from candles, lit jack-o'-lanterns and fire pits.

How much fleece do I need for a hooded cape?

About 2 yards (1.8 m) of 60-inch-wide fleece is enough for an adult knee-length hooded cape, including both hood pieces. A hip-length cape or a child's cape needs about 1.5 yards (1.4 m), and a short elbow-length capelet with a hood needs roughly 1 yard (1 m). Measure the wearer first and add a little extra.

Do you need to hem fleece?

No. Polar fleece does not fray, so cut edges can be left raw on a cape and its hood. Hemming is only for looks: fold the front edges back and topstitch them, or add a blanket stitch in contrast yarn.

Can I make a hooded cape without sewing?

Yes. Glue the hood's back seam and the neckline with fabric glue or fusible web suitable for synthetics, and let it dry fully under weight. Sew the closure on with a few hand stitches, because adhesive closures peel off fleece. Our no-sew cloak guide shows the glue methods in detail.

What is the difference between a cape and a cloak?

The words are often used for the same thing. In costume sewing, a cape is usually shorter and often cut from a rectangle, while a cloak is longer and fuller, cut as a half or full circle. Both can have a hood.

A fleece hooded cape is one of the easiest costume pieces to sew and one of the most useful, since it works for Halloween and keeps you warm on cold evenings afterwards.
