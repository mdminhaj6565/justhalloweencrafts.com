Friday the 13th Crafts: Black Cats, Lucky Charms and Spooky Fun

By Just Halloween Crafts. Published November 4, 2026

The best Friday the 13th crafts play with the old superstitions in a fun way: black cats you can make from a sheet of card, four-leaf clovers built from paper hearts, salt dough lucky charms and a "lucky 13" garland. Most of them use paper, glue and paint you already have, and children from about four can join in with a little help.

In 2026 the last Friday the 13th falls on 13 November, just two weeks after Halloween, so it's a handy excuse to get the black paper out one more time. Below are five step-by-step projects and a few party ideas.

Why Is Friday the 13th Considered Unlucky?

Nobody knows exactly where the Friday the 13th superstition began, but it seems to combine two older beliefs: that Fridays were unlucky and that the number 13 was unlucky. Britannica notes that the earliest clear written link between the two appears in 19th-century France, and that the number 13 already had a bad name in Norse myth and in the story of the Last Supper, where 13 people sat at the table.

For crafting, the fun is in the symbols that go with it: black cats, horseshoes, four-leaf clovers, crossed fingers and the number 13 itself.

What You Need

  • Black card stock and green card stock or construction paper
  • White, yellow and green paper scraps for eyes and details
  • Scissors, a pencil and a ruler
  • Glue stick and PVA (white) glue
  • Plain flour, table salt and water for salt dough
  • A rolling pin, small cookie cutters or a blunt knife, and a straw
  • Acrylic or poster paint, a fine brush and a black marker
  • String, twine or thin ribbon
  • Optional: googly eyes, pipe cleaners, clear varnish or Mod Podge

1. Folded Paper Black Cat

A folded paper black cat stands up on its own and takes about ten minutes, which makes it the best first project for younger children.

  1. Cut a strip of black card about 8 in × 3 in (20 × 8 cm) and roll it into a tube. Glue the overlap. This is the body.
  2. Cut a circle about 2.5 in (6 cm) across for the head. Snip two small triangles on top for ears, or cut the ears as part of the circle.
  3. Glue on yellow or green paper eyes with a black slit for each pupil, plus a tiny pink triangle nose.
  4. Cut a long, curvy tail from black card and glue it inside the back of the tube so it curls up behind the cat.
  5. Glue the head to the top front of the tube.

A cardboard tube painted black works just as well. Our toilet paper roll Halloween crafts use the same idea for bats and monsters.

2. Black Cat Window Silhouettes

Black cat silhouettes are the quickest way to decorate a window for Friday the 13th, because a cat shape reads clearly from the street even when it's cut freehand.

  1. On black card, draw a sitting cat: an oval body, a smaller circle on top for the head, two pointed ears and a long tail curling round the base.
  2. Cut out the shape. For a row of cats, fold a long strip of paper like a fan and draw a cat that touches both folded edges at the tail and ear, then cut through all layers.
  3. Stick the cats to the inside of the window with small loops of tape or a little removable putty.

Mix them with birds from our free printable crow silhouettes for a full spooky skyline.

Tip: Draw the cat on the back of the card in pencil and cut it out from that side. Any pencil marks stay hidden when the cat goes up in the window.

3. Four-Leaf Clover from Paper Hearts

A four-leaf clover is simply four hearts with their points meeting in the middle, so this is an easy way to teach children how shapes combine.

  1. Fold a small square of green paper in half and cut half a heart along the fold. Open it out. Make four hearts the same size.
  2. Arrange the hearts on a work surface with the points touching in the center, like a plus sign.
  3. Cut a thin, slightly curved stem from darker green paper.
  4. Glue the hearts and stem onto a card circle, or glue them to each other where they overlap.
  5. Write "Good luck!" on the back and give it to a friend.

Make clovers in several sizes and string them together.

4. Salt Dough Lucky Charms

Salt dough lets you make lasting lucky charms such as horseshoes, clovers and the number 13, which you can paint and hang on a cord or a mini wreath.

  1. Mix 1 cup of plain flour with ½ cup of table salt, then stir in about ½ cup of water a little at a time until you have a smooth, firm dough.
  2. Knead briefly, roll it about ¼ in (6 mm) thick and cut shapes with small cutters, or by hand with a blunt knife: horseshoes, hearts for clovers, stars, little cats and the numbers 1 and 3.
  3. Press a straw through the top of each charm to make a hanging hole.
  4. Bake on a lined tray at a low temperature, around 250°F (120°C), until hard and dry right through. Thin charms may take two hours; thicker ones take longer. Or let them air-dry for a few days, turning them each day.
  5. Once cool, paint them. Silver or grey works for horseshoes, green for clovers, black with yellow eyes for cats.
  6. Seal with clear varnish or Mod Podge so they don't absorb moisture.

Thread the charms onto twine with a knot between each one, or hang them from a small branch. Add the horseshoe with the open end pointing up, which is the way many people hang them "to keep the luck in".

5. Lucky 13 Paper Garland

A lucky 13 garland is a string of exactly 13 shapes, mixing the "unlucky" and "lucky" symbols for a playful decoration.

  1. Cut 13 shapes about 4 in (10 cm) tall from card: black cats, horseshoes, clovers, crossed fingers and a couple of number 13s.
  2. Punch two holes near the top of each shape.
  3. Thread them onto string or ribbon, weaving in and out of the holes so they stay facing forward.
  4. Hang the garland across a doorway or along a shelf.

Tissue paper tassels between the shapes make it fuller. Our tissue paper garland tutorial shows how to make them.

Variations and Ideas for a Small Party

These crafts work well as a two- or three-station craft table for an after-school get-together.

  • Black cat pom poms: use the method from our bat pom pom tutorial with black yarn, then add paper ears and a tail.
  • Painted lucky stones: paint clovers, horseshoes or tiny cats on smooth stones, like our painted Halloween rocks.

Safety Notes

Salt dough is not food. It contains a lot of salt, which is harmful to dogs and other pets if they eat it, so keep charms and leftover dough out of their reach and hang finished pieces up high. An adult should handle the oven and sharp scissors, and use paper eyes instead of googly eyes or beads for toddlers, as small parts are a choking hazard.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are some easy Friday the 13th crafts for kids?

Good choices are a folded paper black cat, a four-leaf clover made from four paper hearts, and a lucky 13 paper garland. All three use card, scissors and glue, take 10 to 20 minutes and can be done by children from about four, with an adult helping to cut and fold the trickier parts.

When is the next Friday the 13th?

In 2026, Friday the 13th falls in February, March and November. After 13 November 2026, the next one is on Friday 13 August 2027, followed by Friday 13 October 2028. Every year has at least one Friday the 13th and never more than three, depending on which day of the week the year starts.

Why are black cats linked with bad luck?

In parts of Europe, black cats became associated with witches in folklore, which gave them an unlucky reputation that has stuck around. Elsewhere the opposite belief exists: in Britain and Japan, for example, a black cat is often seen as a sign of good luck. Either way, they make a great craft subject.

How long do salt dough charms last?

Fully dried and sealed salt dough charms can last for years if you keep them dry. Moisture is the main problem, as unsealed dough softens and can grow mold in damp rooms. Seal both sides and store them in a box with tissue paper.
