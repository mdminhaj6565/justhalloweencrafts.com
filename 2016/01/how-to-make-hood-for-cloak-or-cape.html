How to Make a Hood for a Cloak or Cape Tutorial

By M. W. Johnstone. Published January 6, 2016. Updated October 11, 2026

Need to make a quick hood for a cloak or cape? Here's my DIY tutorial outlining how I made one when I was asked to make a hooded cloak in a hurry.

The hardest part of making the cloak was constructing the hood for it and then attaching it onto the cape once done. This post will walk you through how to make a quick DIY costume hood without a pattern and then it will lead on to another article which will explain how you can sew it on to the cape. 

It may be that you already have a plain cape like a black one which is often used at Halloween and you want to know how to make a hooded section for it. So this post with photos will explain what you need to do.

How to Make a Hood for a Cloak or Cape Tutorial

When my daughter came home from school and asked for a hooded cloak for a dressing up event, I was very short on time to sew one up and I also had no available pattern that I could follow.

Luckily I had a large piece of thick fabric which was ideal for what she needed. So I took some measurements and got to work.

1: Taking Hood Measurements and a No-Measure Method

Measurement A: First you need to take some measurements from the person who will wear this before you can start making the hood.

Loosely drape a measuring tape from the top of one shoulder up to the top midpoint of the head. The measuring tape should drape a little loosely to mimic how a hood will sit across one side - it definitely doesn’t want to be tight.

Add on an inch to this measurement to allow for a seam allowance and call this Measurement A.
Measurement B: Next you need to take another measurement to work out how much space you need from the front of the hood which comes over the top of your eyes to the back of your head.

You can make this short so your hood shows off the front of your hair or you can make this measurement long so it looks more mysterious and starts to hide your eyes in shadows. Bear this in mind and measure from the top of your head right at the back section to the top of your forehead.

Then add on any extra amount you want for the hood to extend over your eyes plus about an inch for seam allowance. If in doubt, go a little bigger than smaller. Call this Measurement B.

Measurement C: Your final measurement is around your neck. Measure very loosely around the neck area, halve this measurement and add on an inch for seam allowance. This is Measurement C.

No-Measure Method: A cheats way of drawing the hood onto fabric without taking any measurements is simply to grab a hoodie that you wear, fold it in half vertically so you get the shape of the hood and draw around it onto a piece of folded fabric with the top of the hood against the fold of the material. That gives you a rough hood shape to work with and no measuring needed!

2: Drawing the Hood Shape onto Fabric

Using your measurements, you draw your hood shape onto a piece of folded fabric. In the photo above, the fabric is folded at the top. I measured down from the top fold several times in a straight line with Measurement A and marked this off using tailor's chalk. This makes a line going horizontally across the fabric.

Then I used Measurement B across this horizontal line and marked this off. I then marked this position off at several points going up the fabric to make a vertical line. You should now have a rectangular shape marked off on your fabric.

Use Measurement C as follows. Measure from the bottom right corner of the rectangle across to the left on this line and mark off Measurement C. This is the collar area. Draw a curved line from this measurement up to the top of the folded fabric as shown outlined in yellow on the photo.

Carefully cut out this pattern with the fabric folded.

3: Sewing Up the Raw Edges of Fabric

Once you have this cut out, unfold the fabric. Sew a quick overcast stitch or zigzag stitch over all the raw edges to stop the fabric from fraying.

Then down the long straight edge, fold this back from the front to the reverse of the fabric as shown around 1/4 to 1/2 inch or so (whatever you feel more comfortable sewing) and sew down with a straight stitch.

4: Pinning the Cloak Hood

Fold the hood in half with the right sides of the fabric together on the inside and the reverse of the fabric on the outside as shown. Match this up nicely then pin down the curved side as shown.

5: Hood Ready to Sew on to Your Cape or Cloak

Sew from the top of this curve to the bottom with either a 1/4 or 1/2 inch seam and then turn it inside out to reveal a very quick makeshift hood made without a pattern.

It sounds more complicated than it is. Once you understand the measurements you need to take to form this, it becomes very easy. As I said, a good cheat's method is to look for an existing hood and draw around the flattened shape.

Hood Size Tips Before You Cut

Updated October 2026: extra sizing tips and answers to common questions, added by the Just Halloween Crafts team.

The three measurements above work for children and adults alike, but hoods are forgiving, so it is worth spending two minutes on a test before cutting into good fabric.

  • Make a paper mock-up first. Draw the hood shape on newspaper or wrapping paper, tape the back curve together and try it on. If the front edge sits on the forehead and the paper does not pull at the neck, the size is right.
  • Go bigger rather than smaller. A roomy hood drapes in soft folds and looks more dramatic. A tight hood pulls the cloak backwards and slides off the head.
  • Allow for hair and hats. Add a little extra to Measurement B if the wearer has a bun, long thick hair, or will wear a hat or horns underneath.
  • Thick fabric needs more ease. Velvet, fleece and heavy curtain fabric take up more room in the seams than cotton, so add an extra half inch (1 cm) or so to each measurement.

How do I make a pointed hood?

Extend the curved back line upwards into a gentle point instead of rounding it off at the top corner. The longer the point, the more it will flop over, which suits elf, wizard and witch costumes. Try the shape in paper first, because a point that looks small on the table can look very long once it is worn.

Can I line the hood?

Yes. Cut the hood twice, once in your main fabric and once in a lining such as satin or cotton. Sew the back seam of each piece separately, then place them right sides together and stitch along the front opening. Turn the hood right side out, press the front edge and treat both layers as one when you attach it to the cape. A contrast lining, such as red inside a black hood, is an easy way to make a simple costume look special.

Can I make a hood without a sewing machine?

You can. Hand sew the back seam with a small backstitch, which is strong enough for a costume. If you use fleece or felt, the edges will not fray, so you can skip hemming the front opening altogether.
