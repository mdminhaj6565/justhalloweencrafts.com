Free No Trick or Treating Signs Printable

By M. W. Johnstone. Published September 21, 2016. Updated October 7, 2026

This post contains affiliate links. If you buy through them, the site may earn a small commission at no extra cost to you. Disclosure.

There are many reasons why you may not want trick or treaters to call this Halloween. It doesn't mean you're a humbug and hate this Fall season occasion. Whatever the reason, here are 2 free no trick or treat signs that are printable.

All you need to do is save these free printables to your own device and print them off or take them to a print shop to be printed for you. Included is a grim reaper sign and a Fall season one. They will work for standard letter size, A4 paper or smaller.

Perhaps you have a baby who you don't want to get woken up with the doorbell being rung over and over. You may want to put this up to help protect a vulnerable or elderly family member. Or you may just be away from home.

Free No Trick or Treating Printable Signs With Instructions on How to Use Them This Halloween

The free printable posters below are saved as a standard .jpg photo file which makes it very easy for you to download and print.

If you have a Windows computer, when you tap on the file it will probably open up in the photo gallery. From there you click on Print at the top. If it opens up via another program, there should be a print function somewhere.

Image Credit: photo belongs to the author.

The photo above is what I get when I hit print. I can choose to print it as a full page photo on a standard sheet of paper.

This will use more printer ink. If this is going to be a problem, you can opt to print a smaller version where you can add up to 2 copies on one page. This will give a half sheet print which is still a reasonable size to pop on the front door.

The designs print fine on paper but will wrinkle up less if printed on to some card. So use card if you want the final print out to look better. You can laminate the design if it is raining or the weather is damp in order to protect it better.

Other Polite Ways to Show You Are Not Handing Out Treats

A printed sign is the clearest message, but it helps to combine it with a few other signals so trick-or-treaters and their parents understand straight away.

  • Turn off the porch light. In many neighborhoods a dark porch is widely understood to mean "no treats here tonight".
  • Put the sign where it can be seen at child height on the front door or gate, and protect it from rain with a clear plastic sleeve.
  • Leave decorations unlit. Glowing pumpkins and lanterns can look like an invitation, so switch them off for the evening.
  • Tell close neighbors in advance if you are going to be out, so they can let local families know.

Not taking part does not mean missing out on the fun of the season. You can still decorate, make crafts or host a small get-together of your own, such as a Halloween craft party for kids.
